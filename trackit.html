Building notifications for task tracking

Overview

I owned the end-to-end design of a notification systems on a task management platform. This improved workflow efficiency for analysts by 33%. Due to the nature of my NDA, I can not share real working products or names of the project. Therefore, the information in here has been obfuscated and re-created to be more general. Please reach out if you are interested in hearing more.

Problem

Users received notifications across multiple touchpoints, making it difficult to keep track of updates and determine which required immediate attention. Important alerts were easily overlooked, while less critical messages created unnecessary noise. The experience lacked clear prioritization and efficient ways to review or act on notifications.

Solution & Impact

For the system, I created notifications, from low-fis to high-fis. It was part of the iniative to make analyst workflows 33% faster and more efficient than before. Here's what I did: 

  1. Create a single source of truth for notifications
  2. Helped users quickly get to high-priority updates
  3. Reduce cognitive load through clear visual hierarchy
  4. Design a scalable system to accommodate new notifications over the long-term roadmap

Phase 1: Discovery

Notifications are part of the bigger iniative to improve analyst workflows.

Background

For this project, I collaborated with 2 other designers and 1 UX researcher to learn about the goals of the notification center. This was a 0 to 1 project for modernizing the workflow for hundreds of analysts. I was brought on to help bring notifications, one specific feature, in end-to-end.

Taking control of the inbox

Using existing research and interviews from UX researchers, I geared my design to solve for these three areas:

  1. Users need to be alerted to urgent messages as soon as possible.
  2. Users planned to spend most of their time in this platform even at the MVP stage.
  3. For our specific users, there are specific key details that users expect every notification to have (e.g., owner, trigger, & status)

In short, notifications have prioritize for urgency, be informative, & templatize specific deatils

Phase 2: Design & Test

Working in an agile environment for incremental improvements

As I worked on the designs from low-fi to high-fi, I delivered a set of prototypes to test for users each month. I collaborated with our user researcher in an agile manner to get the right questions asked. I worked incrementally to integrate design decisions into the prototype. Here were some of the key insights that we discovered in the process:

  1. Users cared about actionable notifications (e.g., You need to fix a failure) and not so much passive notifications (e.g., Automated action was a success.)
  2. Users preferred a simple inbox interface, instead of one with a lot of functionality (e.g., 5+ filters & ability to favorite)
  3. Users had a specific order to prioritize most notifications and discoverability was the most important, beyond knowing what the notification was.

The key insights helped me improve the notifications design so that users are able to address the most important ones to them, with the correct information that matches their user mental model.

Phase 3: Delivery & impact

Hand-off

To ensure a smooth transition from design to development, I documented the notification system’s components, states, behaviors, and interaction patterns for engineering handoff. I provided clear specifications and usage guidance for different notification types, helping the team maintain consistency across the product while supporting future additions to the system. This documentation also created a shared reference point for designers and developers as the notification experience evolved.
